# Polyphony (many voices)

*The structural move, not the Polyphony protocol.*

## Use when
- A question is best understood from several positions at once (author, publication, reader, node operator).
- The author wants the text itself to model disagreement without a winner.

## Don't use when
- A white paper with a single impersonal voice. This was ruled out for the Polyphony paper's body.
- The voices would have to be invented personas.

## Conflicts with
- `address: impersonal` with a single voice; [thesis-up-top](../document/thesis-up-top.md).

## Tags
`presence: invisible`

## Safe or adds
**Adds.** Voices and their positions are new material.

## The move
Give each position its own short passage, in its own terms, at equal length. Do not adjudicate between them inside the passages. Let a final passage say what they share and where they still differ.

## Before
> To a reader, a publication is still a name that resolves and a page with a byline.

## After
> For the author, a publication is a place their work is offered and can be withdrawn. For the holder, it is a name to keep worth joining. For the reader, it is still a name that resolves and a page with a byline. [...]

*(Each perspective must be checked against the source; the author's and holder's views here are paraphrase.)*

## Pitfalls
- Voices that are all the author in costume.
- Losing the argument in the chorus.

## Combines with
[steelman-dialectic](steelman-dialectic.md), [held-pair](../section/held-pair.md)
