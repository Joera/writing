# Everyday analogy

## Use when
- A technical mechanism has a close everyday equivalent the reader already understands (a key, a lease, a landlord, a notary).
- Figuration allows it. Under `figuration: literal`, this move is out.

## Don't use when
- The analogy breaks at the point that matters.
- It brings a frame the text argues against.

## Conflicts with
- `figuration: literal`; [carry-the-image](../paragraph/carry-the-image.md) in the same passage.

## Tags
`figuration: everyday analogy`

## Safe or adds
**Adds.**

## The move
Give the analogy in one sentence, right after the technical statement. Then say in one sentence where it stops holding.

## Before
> Ending an agreement is a single act with immediate effect. The author does not ask, and the publication does not need to agree.

## After
> Ending an agreement is a single act with immediate effect. It is less like asking a landlord to end a lease than like taking your name off a door you own. The author does not ask, and the publication does not need to agree.

*(Out under the Polyphony paper's literal figuration; shown for other documents.)*

## Pitfalls
- Analogies that need their own explanation.
- More than one analogy for the same thing.
