# Summary time

## Use when
- The text needs to cover a long span (years of a dispute, the history of a practice) in a paragraph.
- The reader needs the shape of what happened, not the scenes.

## Don't use when
- The source does not contain the history. Inventing it is fabrication.
- A real case would become more identifiable with a timeline (the session removed "a year and a half" from case 1 for this reason).

## Conflicts with
- [cases-first](../document/cases-first.md) when the cases are told as scenes: one passage is either scene or summary.

## Tags
`presence: invisible`

## Safe or adds
**Adds.** Propose in the plan.

## The move
Tell a long stretch of time in a few sentences, at a steady distance, moving forward by seasons or years rather than moments. Name what changed at each step; skip what didn't.

## Before
> The company behind it adjusts its terms a little at a time

## After
> In the first year the terms were generous. In the second a clause changed. In the third, the payout schedule. By the fifth, the writers were working under an arrangement none of them had agreed to.

*(The year-by-year structure is new material.)*

## Pitfalls
- Dates and durations that make real parties identifiable.
- Summary that reads as invented history.

## Combines with
[stage-model](../section/stage-model.md), [historical-frame](historical-frame.md)

Robinson (summary time; *The Ministry for the Future*, the Mars trilogy).
