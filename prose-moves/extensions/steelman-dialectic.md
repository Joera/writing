# Steelman dialectic

## Use when
- A design question has two or more serious positions, and the text should show it understands the strongest form of each.
- The author is willing to leave the question without a winner.

## Don't use when
- The text has decided, and a dialectic would pretend otherwise.

## Conflicts with
- [thesis-up-top](../document/thesis-up-top.md) on the same question; [remedy-list](../section/remedy-list.md).

## Tags
`certainty: open, holds ambiguity` (or calibrated).

## Safe or adds
**Adds.** Positions not in the source are new material.

## The move
State each position as its best advocate would. Give each its strongest evidence. Then show what each sees that the other misses. End with what the text does about it, which may be "this paper does not decide".

## Before
> How many governors there are and at what threshold they act are choices for those who run the protocol, and this paper does not set them.

## After
> One view holds that a protocol should be frozen as soon as it works, so no one can bend it. Another holds that a young protocol frozen too early will never become usable. Both are right about something: the first about power, the second about maturity. This paper does not set the number of governors or their threshold. It commits only to the choices being explicit and public.

## Pitfalls
- A dialectic where one side is obviously the author's.
- Dialectic without end, so the reader learns nothing.
