# Historical frame

## Use when
- A present problem has a well-documented historical parallel that clarifies its mechanism (enclosure of the commons; serfs bound to the land; Ostrom's commons institutions).
- The parallel explains, rather than decorates.

## Don't use when
- The parallel carries politics the author doesn't want (techno-feudalism is credited to Varoufakis and carries his argument with it).
- It would need more than a paragraph to explain.

## Conflicts with
- `figuration: literal` if the parallel is used as an image rather than as evidence.

## Tags
`persuasion: mechanism`

## Safe or adds
**Adds.** Historical material is new; it must be accurate and credited.

## The move
Name the historical case in a sentence, say what mechanism it shared with the present, and say where the parallel breaks. Credit the source of the framing if it is someone's argument.

## Before
> The party with less to lose sets the terms

## After
> Medieval serfs were not owned. They were bound to the land: free in law, unable in practice to leave it without losing their living. Writers bound to a masthead are in a milder version of the same position. The parallel ends there: a writer can still walk away; they just can't take the work.

*(New material; would need the author's approval.)*

## Pitfalls
- Historical inaccuracy.
- A parallel that flatters the argument and stops being checked.
