# Working metaphor

## Use when
- A mechanism has a physical shape that a metaphor can carry (a gate, a fence, a room, rails).
- Figuration allows new images, and the metaphor will be used more than once.

## Don't use when
- `figuration: literal`. Carry existing images instead ([carry-the-image](../paragraph/carry-the-image.md)).
- The metaphor carries baggage (a herd implies the writers are animals).

## Conflicts with
- `figuration: literal`; [everyday-analogy](everyday-analogy.md) and [carry-the-image](../paragraph/carry-the-image.md) in the same passage.

## Tags
`figuration: moderate` or `sparse, precise, central image`.

## Safe or adds
**Adds.**

## The move
Introduce one physical image that matches the mechanism part for part. Use it to explain, not to decorate: each part of the image should correspond to a part of the system. Return to it later instead of introducing a second.

## Before
> What keeps it from being used for anything are the gates

## After
> What keeps it from being used for anything are the gates. Each stands at a fixed point on the road from source to page, and each opens only for what meets its condition. Nobody stands at a gate deciding; the gate is the condition.

*(Polyphony already uses "gates", so there the move is [carry-the-image](../paragraph/carry-the-image.md). A metaphor rejected on baggage: the session's electric fence, which matches the diagnosis part for part, since it seldom has to shock anyone, but makes the writers a herd.)*

## Pitfalls
- Mixed metaphors.
- A metaphor that implies something false about the people in it.

## Combines with
[carry-the-image](../paragraph/carry-the-image.md) (later passages), [coin-the-term](../document/coin-the-term.md)

Robinson (restrained physical metaphor); Le Guin (central image).
