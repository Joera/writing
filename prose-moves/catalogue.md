# Catalogue

Every move and pass on one page. Select on **tags** (see [properties.md](properties.md)); the source column is provenance only. *Use when* is the first condition from each file; read the file before choosing.

## Document moves

| Move | Use when | Tags | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Cases first](document/cases-first.md) | The argument is abstract and the reader has no picture yet of who is affected. | persuasion: mechanism + case | Robinson, Doctorow |
| [Coin the term](document/coin-the-term.md) | The text describes a pattern that has no name, and keeps describing it at length each time. | — | Doctorow |
| [Cost inside](document/cost-inside.md) | The text proposes something, and the proposal has costs, trade-offs or people it serves badly. | certainty: flat, with limits | Le Guin |
| [Mechanism, then consequence](document/mechanism-then-consequence.md) | The text proposes a system, and its claims about outcomes depend on how the system works. | persuasion: mechanism | Robinson |
| [Name the picture first](document/name-the-picture-first.md) | Readers arrive with a picture already in their heads (of a platform, of "decentralised", of a blockchain project) that will distort what follows. | — | Palmer (inferred) |
| [Return to the cases](document/return-to-cases.md) | The text opened on cases (cases-first or thought-experiment) and then left them for mechanism. | persuasion: mechanism + case | Robinson |
| [Thesis up top](document/thesis-up-top.md) | Readers will skim, or stop after the first paragraph (abstracts, summaries, posts). | certainty: flat | Doctorow |
| [Thought experiment](document/thought-experiment.md) | The argument depends on a situation that does not exist yet, or cannot be shown with a real case. | persuasion: thought experiment | Le Guin |
| [Glossary epigraph](document/glossary-epigraph.md) | The document has a glossary, and each section is built around one term. | — | Stephenson (unreviewed) |

## Section moves

| Move | Use when | Tags | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Belief and correction](section/belief-and-correction.md) | A widely held belief stands between the reader and the argument, and the text can show where it goes wrong. | certainty: calibrated | Palmer |
| [Define the term](section/define-the-term.md) | A term carries the argument and is used in more than one sense (in the Polyphony draft, "publication" is defined three ways). | — | Palmer, Doctorow (inferred) |
| [Edges statement](section/edges-statement.md) | A reader could reasonably think the proposal does more than it does. | certainty: flat, with limits | Robinson, Palmer |
| [Held pair](section/held-pair.md) | Two things are both true and pull against each other, and the honest position is to keep both (the protocol must be upgradable while young; an upgradable protocol has governors who could misuse it). | certainty: open, holds ambiguity | Le Guin |
| [Numbered mechanism](section/numbered-mechanism.md) | A mechanism has a fixed number of parts (three rails, two gates, four guarantees), and the reader needs to know when the list is complete. | persuasion: mechanism, register: plain | Robinson |
| [Outsider's view](section/outsiders-view.md) | Something is so familiar to the reader that its strangeness has become invisible (that a website belongs to whoever holds the credentials). | presence: invisible | Le Guin |
| [Recap](section/recap.md) | A long technical stretch has ended and the reader needs to know what they now know before the next part. | register: plain | Doctorow |
| [Reframe](section/reframe.md) | The usual framing of a problem leads to the wrong remedy (groupthink as a topic vs. groupthink as a structure). | — | Doctorow; variant Le Guin |
| [Remedy list](section/remedy-list.md) | The diagnosis is done and the reader asks "so what do we do?". | persuasion: mechanism | Doctorow |
| [Spiral return](section/spiral-return.md) | An idea needs to be understood at more than one depth, and the reader cannot take the full depth on first meeting. | rhythm: periodic | Palmer |
| [Stage model](section/stage-model.md) | A problem develops over time in recognisable steps, and readers have only seen the end state. | persuasion: mechanism | Doctorow |
| [Staged objection](section/staged-objection.md) | An informed reader will raise a specific objection at this point, and the text has an answer. | persuasion: objection | Palmer |
| [Two-step diagnosis](section/two-step-diagnosis.md) | One paragraph currently carries both *what goes wrong* and *why*, and the reader loses the join. | persuasion: mechanism | Robinson |
| [First principles](section/first-principles.md) | A design choice looks arbitrary unless the reader sees the constraint that forces it. | persuasion: mechanism | Stephenson (unreviewed) |

## Paragraph moves

| Move | Use when | Tags | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Build, then land](paragraph/build-then-land.md) | A paragraph accumulates details toward one point, and the point is currently buried in the middle. | rhythm: paratactic | Robinson |
| [Calibrated certainty](paragraph/calibrated-certainty.md) | A paragraph mixes claims of different strength (what the code guarantees; what the authors expect; what no one knows yet), and they are all stated in the same flat voice. | certainty: calibrated | Palmer |
| [Carry the image](paragraph/carry-the-image.md) | The text already uses a concrete image (room, keys, door, rails, gates, chain, paymaster), and a later passage could use it again instead of reaching for a new one. | figuration: literal | Le Guin |
| [Catalogue paragraph](paragraph/catalogue-paragraph.md) | The point is the sheer range or number of things involved (what a writer loses; what a host controls). | rhythm: paratactic, register: plain | Robinson |
| [Direct address](paragraph/direct-address.md) | The document's address is "you" and a paragraph needs the reader to imagine themselves in a position (the writer who wants to leave). | address: "you", presence: visible | Palmer, Doctorow |
| [Fact, qualification, consequence](paragraph/fact-qualification-consequence.md) | A paragraph states a technical fact that has an important exception or limit, and a practical result. | persuasion: mechanism, certainty: flat, with limits | Robinson (inferred) |
| [One before many](paragraph/one-before-many.md) | A paragraph opens with a general rule and only then gives instances, and readers lose the rule before they see what it means. | — | Robinson (inferred) |
| [One-line paragraph](paragraph/one-line-paragraph.md) | One sentence is the turn of the whole section and must not be read as part of the paragraph around it. | rhythm: paratactic, short | Doctorow |
| [Plain close](paragraph/plain-close.md) | A paragraph or section ends on an ornate or abstract sentence, and the real point is simpler. | register: plain | Robinson, Le Guin (inferred) |
| [Qualifying parenthesis](paragraph/qualifying-parenthesis.md) | A claim needs a short qualification that would derail the sentence if given its own clause. | rhythm: periodic | Palmer |
| [Worked attack](paragraph/worked-attack.md) | The text claims a system resists tampering, forgery or capture, and states this as a general guarantee. | persuasion: mechanism | Stephenson (unreviewed) |

## Sentence moves

| Move | Use when | Tags | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Balanced pair](sentence/balanced-pair.md) | A sentence states two things that mirror each other (author and publication; leave and stay), and the mirroring is the point. | — | Le Guin (inferred) |
| [Cadence](sentence/cadence.md) | The last sentence of a section or of the document should land when read aloud. | rhythm: cadenced | Le Guin |
| [Colon as "which means"](sentence/colon-as-which-means.md) | The second half of a sentence explains, specifies or follows from the first. | — | Robinson |
| [Cumulative sentence](sentence/cumulative-sentence.md) | A main clause needs several details attached, and the reader should get the point first and the detail after. | rhythm: paratactic | Robinson |
| [Hinge connectives](sentence/hinge-connectives.md) | The logic between sentences is real but unmarked, so the reader has to infer "because" or "so". | register: plain | Robinson |
| [Periodic sentence](sentence/periodic-sentence.md) | A condition must be understood before the result can be (gate conditions: "only when X, and only while Y, may Z"). | rhythm: periodic | Palmer |
| [Plain diction](sentence/plain-diction.md) | A sentence uses abstract, Latinate or jargon words where plain ones exist. | register: plain | Robinson, Le Guin |
| [Question, then answer](sentence/question-then-answer.md) | A paragraph answers a question the reader has but the text never states. | — | Palmer, Doctorow (inferred) |
| [Term discipline](sentence/term-discipline.md) | Always, once a term is defined or coined. This move is less a choice than a rule. | — | Doctorow |
| [Triad](sentence/triad.md) | There are genuinely three items, and listing them in rising order of weight helps the last land. | — | unattributed in the record |

## Extensions (adds: author approval required)

| Move | Use when | Tags | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Aside](extensions/aside.md) | The writer is visible, and a short personal remark, admission of bias, or tangent builds trust or clarity. | presence: visible, register: colloquial | Doctorow, Palmer |
| [Concrete figures](extensions/concrete-figures.md) | A claim about scale or cost would be more convincing with a number (how many outlets, what a render costs, how many nodes). | persuasion: mechanism | Robinson |
| [Everyday analogy](extensions/everyday-analogy.md) | A technical mechanism has a close everyday equivalent the reader already understands (a key, a lease, a landlord, a notary). | figuration: everyday analogy | Doctorow |
| [Historical frame](extensions/historical-frame.md) | A present problem has a well-documented historical parallel that clarifies its mechanism (enclosure of the commons; serfs bound to the land; Ostrom's commons institutions). | persuasion: mechanism | Robinson |
| [Polyphony (many voices)](extensions/polyphony.md) | A question is best understood from several positions at once (author, publication, reader, node operator). | presence: invisible | Robinson |
| [Steelman dialectic](extensions/steelman-dialectic.md) | A design question has two or more serious positions, and the text should show it understands the strongest form of each. | certainty: open, holds ambiguity | Robinson, Palmer |
| [Summary time](extensions/summary-time.md) | The text needs to cover a long span (years of a dispute, the history of a practice) in a paragraph. | presence: invisible | Robinson |
| [Working metaphor](extensions/working-metaphor.md) | A mechanism has a physical shape that a metaphor can carry (a gate, a fence, a room, rails). | figuration: moderate | Robinson, Le Guin |
| [Explanatory digression](extensions/explanatory-digression.md) | The argument depends on a technical idea that many readers don't know. | — | Stephenson (unreviewed) |
| [Sustained conceit](extensions/sustained-conceit.md) | A whole comparison maps closely onto an everyday one, part for part. | figuration: sustained conceit | Stephenson (unreviewed) |

## Passes (run on every text)

| Pass | Checks | Lens / source |
|---|---|---|
| [Claim inventory](passes/claim-inventory.md) | What the source text actually claims. | Robinson rewrite rule from the session |
| [Abstraction audit](passes/abstraction-audit.md) | *Is the mechanism actually explained?* Finds sentences that name an outcome (integrity, independence, trust, sovereignty) without the steps that produce it. | Robinson's lens |
| [Term consistency](passes/term-consistency.md) | *Are the key ideas named, and do the terms drift?* Finds key terms used in more than one sense, and ideas referred to by more than one word. | Doctorow's lens |
| [Objection check](passes/objection-check.md) | *Which objections are unanswered?* Finds the questions an informed, sceptical reader would raise at each point, and whether the text answers them. | Palmer's lens |
| [Register check](passes/register-check.md) | That diction, address and presence match the document settings everywhere. | unattributed in the record |
| [Epigram audit](passes/epigram-audit.md) | That short, quotable sentences say something a reader can understand on first reading. | lessons from the Polyphony naming work |
| [Claim sourcing](passes/claim-sourcing.md) | *What does each claim rest on?* For every claim in the claim inventory, whether the text gives its basis: the mechanism (a section of this text), evidence (a source, a case), or nothing (an assertion). | added with Doctorow; Palmer's lens |
| [Read aloud](passes/read-aloud.md) | *How does it sound?* Finds sentences that trip, run out of breath, bury their stress, or repeat a sound or rhythm by accident. | Le Guin's lens |
| [Crowding and leaping](passes/crowding-and-leaping.md) | Whether each passage includes what it needs and leaves out what it doesn't. | Le Guin, Steering the Craft |
| [Presupposition](passes/presupposition.md) | *What does the language take for granted?* Finds words and constructions that slip in a claim the text never makes, or a frame the text means to reject. | Le Guin's lens |
| [Fidelity check](passes/fidelity-check.md) | That the rewrite says what the source said: every claim present, at the same strength and scope, and nothing added that the author did not approve. | session rule |
| [Attack surface](passes/attack-surface.md) | Could someone break it, and does the text say why they can't? | Stephenson (unreviewed) |
