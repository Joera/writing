# Name the picture first

## Use when
- Readers arrive with a picture already in their heads (of a platform, of "decentralised", of a blockchain project) that will distort what follows.
- The text's argument depends on replacing that picture.

## Don't use when
- The reader has no prior picture; naming one plants it.
- The picture can only be named by caricaturing people who hold it.

## Conflicts with
- [thesis-up-top](thesis-up-top.md) when both would claim the first paragraph. Choose which the reader needs first.

## Tags
None. Works under any setting.

## Safe or adds
Safe, if the picture is one the text already argues against. Adds, if it introduces a misconception the text does not address.

## The move
Before explaining, say plainly what the reader probably already imagines, in its own terms and without mockery. Then say which part of that picture holds and which does not. The explanation that follows replaces only the part that does not.

## Before
> Polyphony is a protocol, not a platform: a platform has an owner, and an owner can become the next place nobody can leave.

## After
> Most new publishing tools are platforms: a company runs them, and writers move in. Polyphony is a protocol instead. A platform has an owner, and an owner can become the next place nobody can leave.

## Pitfalls
- Naming a straw picture nobody holds.
- Spending longer on the wrong picture than on the right one.
- Repeating the wrong picture so vividly that it is what the reader remembers.
