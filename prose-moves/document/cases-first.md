# Cases first

## Use when
- The argument is abstract and the reader has no picture yet of who is affected.
- The text already contains (or the author supplies) concrete situations that the argument explains.
- Several different situations share one outcome, and that shared outcome is the thesis.

## Don't use when
- The reader already knows the problem and wants the mechanism (a specification, a reference section).
- The cases would have to be invented. Use [thought-experiment](thought-experiment.md) instead, and say it is one.
- The text is short enough that the thesis fits in its first two sentences: use [thesis-up-top](thesis-up-top.md).

## Conflicts with
- [thesis-up-top](thesis-up-top.md) for the same opening. One text can open on cases and still put a thesis in the abstract.

## Tags
`persuasion: mechanism + case`. Works under any address and register.

## Safe or adds
Safe, if the cases come from the source. Adds, if any case is new.

## The move
Open on two or three concrete situations, each told in a few sentences, before naming the problem. Make them different on the surface and identical in outcome. Then state the shared outcome once, as the hinge into diagnosis. Keep the cases parallel in length and shape so the reader sees the pattern without being told to look for it.

## Before
> Online publishing today answers all three the same way: someone holds the keys. [preceded by an abstract statement that publishing runs on dependency]

## After
> Two journalists work a beat together for years. [...] A group of writers runs a publication on a platform that promised to stay out of the way. [...] News breaks on a subject perhaps forty people in the world understand. [...]
>
> Online publishing today answers all three the same way: someone holds the keys.

## Pitfalls
- Cases so long the reader forgets they are evidence.
- Cases that differ in outcome, so the hinge sentence is false for one of them.
- Real cases that identify real people or make an unresolved accusation. Strip dates, sums and names until the parties would not obviously recognise themselves.
- A diagnosis that only explains one of the cases.
