# Coin the term

## Use when
- The text describes a pattern that has no name, and keeps describing it at length each time.
- The argument needs readers to carry an idea out of the text and repeat it.
- A problem and its remedy can be named as a pair (unison / polyphony).

## Don't use when
- An existing term already does the job; use it and credit it (techno-feudalism: Varoufakis).
- The name needs explaining before it makes sense ("stay by choice").
- The name is clever rather than clear ("enblandification", "beigification").

## Conflicts with
None as a move. A coined term then binds [term-discipline](../sentence/term-discipline.md) for the rest of the text.

## Tags
None.

## Safe or adds
Safe in content (it names something already argued), but the name itself is a choice the author must approve.

## The move
Give the pattern a short, plain name. Introduce it once, with a sentence that says what it means, at the moment the reader has just seen the thing it names. Then use it, unchanged, every time. A good name is a name and a story: the name, the mechanism behind it, a case that shows it, the remedy, and repetition.

Test a candidate before keeping it:
- Does it locate the cause where the text does? ("Unison worldview" puts the cause in belief; the text puts it in the price of leaving.)
- Does it carry baggage? ("Censor" invokes the state; "UnHerd" is taken and politically coded.)
- Would someone who has not read the text understand it in one sentence?
- Does it say the good news, or a doubled loss? ("Costs both sides" reads as worse; "makes staying a choice" reads as the remedy.)

## Before
> What accumulates is not a policy but a disposition — a publication that speaks with one voice and can point to no instance of having enforced it.

## After
> What accumulates is not a policy but a disposition: a publication that speaks with one voice and can point to no instance of having enforced it. Call it **unison media**.

## Pitfalls
- Coining more terms than the reader can hold. One or two per text.
- Coining, then drifting: run [term-consistency](../passes/term-consistency.md).
- A name for the cause offered as a name for the effect, or the other way round.
