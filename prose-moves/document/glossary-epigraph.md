# Glossary epigraph

## Use when
- The document has a glossary, and each section is built around one term.
- Readers jump into sections out of order and need the term fixed before they read.

## Don't use when
- There is no settled glossary yet. Fix terms first.
- Sections are not organised around single terms.

## Conflicts with
- [define-the-term](../section/define-the-term.md) in the same section: the epigraph is the definition, so don't define again in the body.

## Tags
None.

## Safe or adds
Safe, if the entry is copied from the document's own glossary.

## The move
Open each section with the glossary entry for its key term, set apart (indented, italic, or as a quoted dictionary entry). Then start the body without restating it. The entry stays exactly as it reads in the glossary, so the text and the glossary cannot drift apart.

## Before
> ### 4. Agreements
> An agreement is between an author and a publication, over a single work. It records who wrote it, that the publication may render it, and how revenue divides between them. It is entered by both parties and can be ended by either.
>
> Agreements are recorded on a public ledger: [...]

## After
> ### 4. Agreements
> > **Agreement.** The arrangement between one author and one publication over one work: that the publication may render it, and how revenue divides. Either side can end it.
>
> Agreements are recorded on a public ledger: [...]

## Pitfalls
- An epigraph that differs from the glossary by a word.
- Epigraphs on sections that aren't about one term, so the entry feels decorative.
