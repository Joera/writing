# Cost inside

## Use when
- The text proposes something, and the proposal has costs, trade-offs or people it serves badly.
- Those costs are currently in a separate "limitations" section at the end, or missing.
- Credibility matters more than momentum.

## Don't use when
- The text is a specification with no persuasive intent.
- The cost would have to be invented. Use [passes/presupposition.md](../passes/presupposition.md) to find real ones.

## Conflicts with
- [thesis-up-top](thesis-up-top.md) only if the thesis is polemical (certainty: flat, polemical). A flat thesis with limits can carry a cost.

## Tags
`certainty: flat, with limits` or `open, holds ambiguity`.

## Safe or adds
Safe, when the cost is stated or implied in the source. Adds, when it is newly identified: propose it.

## The move
Put each cost inside the argument it belongs to, at the point where the benefit is claimed, rather than in a list at the end. State it in the same plain voice as the benefit. Do not apologise for it and do not argue it away in the same sentence.

## Before
> An author who leaves takes [...] their accumulated, already-published work off the publication's live vouched record [...] that corpus is a loss no contributor can refill.

## After
> An author who leaves takes their published work off the publication's record, and the name stops vouching for it. The publication loses work no newcomer can replace. The author loses the masthead that vouched for it. Leaving still costs both sides something; what changes is that neither side holds the other's work.

## Pitfalls
- A cost so softened it reads as a benefit.
- Costs only for the other side.
- Collecting the costs at the end anyway, as a list, so the body reads as unqualified.
