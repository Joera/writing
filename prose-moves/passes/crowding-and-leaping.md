# Crowding and leaping

## What it checks
Whether each passage includes what it needs and leaves out what it doesn't. **Crowding** is packing in detail and qualification until the point is lost. **Leaping** is skipping a step the reader needed. Good prose does both deliberately; bad prose does the wrong one in the wrong place.

## When to run
After rewriting, on any passage that feels either dense or thin.

## How
1. For each paragraph, name its one job.
2. Crowding: cross out every phrase that does not serve that job. If the paragraph still does its job, the phrases were crowding. Check them against the [claim inventory](claim-inventory.md) before cutting: a claim must move, not vanish.
3. Crowding also covers **repetition as padding** and **the unbroken exposition lump** ([anti-patterns 2 and 3](../anti-patterns.md)): a point made again without adding anything, and explanation running past one paragraph without a break into units.
4. Leaping: between each pair of sentences, ask whether a reader could get from one to the next. If a step is missing, find it in the source or flag it.

## Output
Per paragraph: crowded (phrases to cut or move) · leaps (missing steps) · fine.

## Example
- Abstract: crowded. One sentence carries ownership, governance by threshold, the ledger, the generator, and a definition of "valid". Split, and move "valid" to §6.
- §2 "History is ordered and timestamped. [...] Rotation survival, reputation, and dispute resolution are all consequences of this one property." → leap (the placeholder hides the step). Restore it from the source or flag.
