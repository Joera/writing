# Spiral return

## Use when
- An idea needs to be understood at more than one depth, and the reader cannot take the full depth on first meeting.
- A section returns to a point made earlier, and should now add a layer rather than repeat.

## Don't use when
- The document setting is "each formulation stated once in full". Spiral returns must then add, never restate.
- The text is short.

## Conflicts with
- [recap](recap.md) for the same return: a recap restates; a spiral deepens.

## Tags
`rhythm: periodic` habitually; works in other rhythms if each return adds.

## Safe or adds
Safe.

## The move
Introduce the idea simply. Move on. Come back to it later with what the reader has learned since, and show the idea again at greater depth, pointing back explicitly ("Earlier this paper said ending an agreement is a fact rather than a request. Here is why.").

## Before
> [Introduction] ending one is a fact rather than a request.
> [Section 4] This is what makes ending an agreement a fact rather than a request.

## After
> [Introduction] ending one is a fact rather than a request.
> [Section 4] The introduction called ending an agreement a fact rather than a request. The reason is the record: neither party operates it, neither can rewrite it, and there is no host to pull.

## Pitfalls
- A return that is only a repetition.
- Too many returns, so the reader feels the text going in circles.
