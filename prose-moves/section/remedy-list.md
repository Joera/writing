# Remedy list

## Use when
- The diagnosis is done and the reader asks "so what do we do?".
- The remedies are distinct and each answers a named part of the problem.

## Don't use when
- There is one remedy (state it).
- The remedies are not in the source.

## Conflicts with
- [held-pair](held-pair.md) as the closing of the same section: a remedy list resolves; a held pair leaves tension open.

## Tags
`persuasion: mechanism`

## Safe or adds
Safe if every remedy is in the source.

## The move
List the remedies, each matched to the part of the problem it answers. Lead each with a verb. Say who can act on it. Keep it short: three to five items.

## Before
> Polyphony fixes both: writers keep their work when they leave, and anyone can start a publication cheaply, alone or with others.

## After
> Polyphony works on both halves of the problem:
> - **Leaving no longer costs the record.** Writers keep their work when they leave.
> - **There is somewhere to go.** Anyone can start a publication cheaply, alone or with others, with the roles (writer, editor, marketer) split however they like.

*(The specialised-roles point is from the session's open-market discussion and still needs its own paragraph in the introduction.)*

## Pitfalls
- Remedies that do not map to the diagnosis.
- A wish list presented as a plan.
