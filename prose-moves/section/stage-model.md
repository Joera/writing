# Stage model

## Use when
- A problem develops over time in recognisable steps, and readers have only seen the end state.
- Showing the sequence makes the outcome look inevitable rather than chosen.

## Don't use when
- The stages are invented to make the story tidy.
- The process is a mechanism with simultaneous parts: use [numbered-mechanism](numbered-mechanism.md).

## Conflicts with
- [numbered-mechanism](numbered-mechanism.md) for the same content.

## Tags
`persuasion: mechanism`

## Safe or adds
Safe if the stages are in the source; adds if the source only describes the end state.

## The move
Name the stages in order (first, then, then, finally), each in one or two sentences, each driven by the one before. Make the incentive at each stage visible. End on the stage the reader recognises from their own experience.

## Before
> The company behind it adjusts its terms a little at a time — a clause, a payout schedule, a content policy — each change too small to leave over, the sum of them an arrangement nobody agreed to.

## After
> First, the platform needs writers, and its terms are generous. Then it has them, and a clause changes, too small to leave over. Then a payout schedule, then a content policy. Each change is small. The sum is an arrangement nobody agreed to.

## Pitfalls
- More than four or five stages.
- Stages that do not follow from one another.
- A stage model presented as fact when it is a pattern.
