# Two-step diagnosis

## Use when
- One paragraph currently carries both *what goes wrong* and *why*, and the reader loses the join.
- The cause is structural (a position, an incentive) rather than a villain.

## Don't use when
- The cause and effect fit in two sentences; splitting them pads.
- The text blames an actor, and the evidence is about that actor's choices.

## Conflicts with
- [reframe](reframe.md) for the same paragraphs: a reframe changes the question, a diagnosis answers it.

## Tags
`persuasion: mechanism`

## Safe or adds
Safe.

## The move
Split the diagnosis into two paragraphs. The first says what happens, in observable terms: who loses what, and who sets the terms. The second says why it happens, as a mechanism no one has to intend. Close the second on the cause stated plainly.

## Before
> Online publishing today answers all three the same way: someone holds the keys. A group of writers can accept a platform's terms [...] A falling-out costs the writer their income, their record, and the home of their byline; it costs the publication one contributor, who can be replaced. The party with less to lose sets the terms, and everyone inside adjusts without being asked to. What accumulates is not a policy but a disposition [...] it follows from the price of leaving.

## After
> Online publishing today answers all three the same way: someone holds the keys. A group of writers can accept a platform's terms, which can change or end without them, or run their own server, which places the site, the list, the archive and the revenue in the hands of whoever holds the credentials. A falling-out costs the writer their income, their record and the home of their byline. It costs the publication one contributor, who can be replaced.
>
> So the party with less to lose sets the terms, and everyone inside adjusts without being asked to. What accumulates is not a policy but a disposition: a publication that speaks with one voice and can point to no instance of having enforced it. No one had to choose that. It follows from the price of leaving.

## Pitfalls
- A second paragraph that restates the first in abstract words instead of giving the cause.
- Making the mechanism sound like a conspiracy.
