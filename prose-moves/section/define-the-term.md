# Define the term

## Use when
- A term carries the argument and is used in more than one sense (in the Polyphony draft, "publication" is defined three ways).
- A familiar word is used in a narrower technical sense (protocol, holder, record).

## Don't use when
- The term is used once.
- A glossary already defines it and the text uses it consistently.

## Conflicts with
None.

## Tags
None.

## Safe or adds
Safe. Choosing *which* of several existing definitions is canonical is an author decision; put it in the plan.

## The move
At the first use that matters, give the definition in one sentence: what it is, then what it includes and excludes. Use the same wording as the glossary. After that, every use must fit the definition ([term-discipline](../sentence/term-discipline.md)). If the term replaces a looser one ("holder" for "owner of the publication"), say so once.

## Before
> A publication is a name, a look, a domain its pages are served from, and an editorial policy. [section 3]
> The set of agreements currently in force is what the publication consists of. [section 4]

## After
> A publication is a name under which work appears: its look, its editorial policy, and the agreements currently in force with its authors. A work is in the publication while an agreement covers it, and out when that agreement ends.

## Pitfalls
- Defining by synonym.
- A definition in the text that differs from the glossary.
- Defining terms the reader already shares, which reads as condescension.
