# Outsider's view

## Use when
- Something is so familiar to the reader that its strangeness has become invisible (that a website belongs to whoever holds the credentials).
- Describing it as an outsider would describe it exposes an assumption the argument depends on.

## Don't use when
- The reader is already an outsider to the subject.
- It would require a fictional observer in a non-fiction document.

## Conflicts with
- [direct-address](../paragraph/direct-address.md) in the same passage: the outsider's eye needs distance.

## Tags
`presence: invisible`

## Safe or adds
Safe if the description stays literal.

## The move
Describe the familiar arrangement plainly, as an ethnographer would describe a custom: who does what, who owns what, what happens when someone leaves, without the words insiders use to make it sound natural. Let the reader notice the oddity; don't point at it.

## Before
> A group of writers can accept a platform's terms, which can change or end without them, or run their own server, which places the site, the subscriber list, the archive, and the revenue in the hands of whoever holds the credentials.

## After
> A group of writers who want to publish together must first decide who will hold the keys. Whoever holds them holds the site, the list of readers, the archive and the money. The others write there as long as the holder agrees.

## Pitfalls
- Faux-naive tone, which reads as sarcasm.
- An "alien observer" device in a white paper.
