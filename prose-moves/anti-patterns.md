# Anti-patterns

Things to avoid in any text, whatever the settings. Recovered from step 6 of the session, where the list was given as Robinson's weaknesses to avoid. Items 5–8 were raised later in the session (steps 12, 16–17, 33–35) and are added so the list is complete.

Each anti-pattern is caught by a pass (or, for style projection, at the plan step). The **Caught by** line says where.

1. **Lecturing.** Exposition that tells the reader what to think rather than showing how something works.
   *Caught by:* [abstraction-audit](passes/abstraction-audit.md).

2. **Repetition as padding.** Teaching by repetition until it stops teaching.
   *Caught by:* [crowding-and-leaping](passes/crowding-and-leaping.md). See also the pitfalls of [recap](section/recap.md) and [spiral-return](section/spiral-return.md).

3. **The unbroken exposition lump.** Explanation delivered as one undivided block; break it into units.
   *Caught by:* [crowding-and-leaping](passes/crowding-and-leaping.md). Typical fixes: [numbered-mechanism](section/numbered-mechanism.md), [two-step-diagnosis](section/two-step-diagnosis.md).

4. **Examples that only carry a point.** Characters or cases that exist only to make an argument and have no life of their own.
   *Caught by:* [presupposition](passes/presupposition.md). See also the pitfalls of [cases-first](document/cases-first.md).

5. **Style projection.** Applying one writer's taste everywhere instead of where the passage's job calls for it (step 17).
   *Caught by:* the plan step in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) (every move must name the passage's job) and [fidelity-check](passes/fidelity-check.md).

6. **Mixed voices in one paragraph.** Parataxis and periodic sentences, or "you" and impersonal address, inside one paragraph (steps 9–10).
   *Caught by:* [register-check](passes/register-check.md).

7. **Cleverness that needs decoding.** Short epigrammatic sentences or coined phrases that force the reader into mental gymnastics; plain three-sentence explanations beat cryptic one-liners (steps 33–35).
   *Caught by:* [epigram-audit](passes/epigram-audit.md).

8. **Announcing the mechanism instead of the benefit.** Phrasing that is accurate but reads as bad news (e.g. "makes leaving cost both sides"); say the good news, leave the mechanism for the unpacking (step 33).
   *Caught by:* [epigram-audit](passes/epigram-audit.md).
