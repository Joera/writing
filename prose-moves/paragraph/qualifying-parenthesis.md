# Qualifying parenthesis

## Use when
- A claim needs a short qualification that would derail the sentence if given its own clause.
- The qualification is real (a scope, an exception, an example), not decoration.

## Don't use when
- The qualification is important enough to be its own sentence.
- The paragraph already has one parenthesis.
- The document avoids em-dashes; use round brackets or commas.

## Conflicts with
- [plain-close](plain-close.md) in the closing sentence: a close is clean.

## Tags
`rhythm: periodic` habitually; works in paratactic prose used sparingly.

## Safe or adds
Safe.

## The move
Put the qualification in round brackets inside the sentence, right after the word it qualifies, and keep it under a dozen words. One per paragraph.

## Before
> It addresses the parties to a publication — the writers, the name, the readers — and not the state

## After
> It addresses the parties to a publication (the writers, the name, the readers) and not the state.

## Pitfalls
- Nested parentheses.
- Using the parenthesis to hide a concession that belongs in the open.
