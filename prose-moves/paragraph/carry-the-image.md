# Carry the image

## Use when
- The text already uses a concrete image (room, keys, door, rails, gates, chain, paymaster), and a later passage could use it again instead of reaching for a new one.
- Figuration is literal or sparse: no new images allowed, but existing ones may travel.

## Don't use when
- The image would have to be stretched to fit.
- The image was dropped from the earlier passage in revision (case 1 lost "the room").

## Conflicts with
- [working-metaphor](../extensions/working-metaphor.md) and [everyday-analogy](../extensions/everyday-analogy.md) in the same passage: carry an old image or add a new one, not both.

## Tags
`figuration: literal` (also sparse, precise, central image).

## Safe or adds
Safe: the image is already in the text.

## The move
Bring the earlier image back, unchanged in meaning, at the point where it explains the new passage. Use the same word. One image per passage.

## Before
> Whoever leaves takes a list and starts over. Whoever stays keeps the reach, and the neighbors. The group splits not over what they believe but over what they can carry out the door.
> [...] Before they can start, someone has to own the room.

## After
> [Case 2] [...] what they can carry out the door.
> [Case 3] [...] Before they can start, someone has to own the room.
> [Closing] [...] The forty would have their room by Friday, and no one would own it.

## Pitfalls
- Mixing images (a door in a fence in a chain).
- Changing what the image means between uses.
