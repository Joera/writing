# Catalogue paragraph

## Use when
- The point is the sheer range or number of things involved (what a writer loses; what a host controls).
- The items are concrete and the reader recognises each.

## Don't use when
- The items need explanation one by one: use [numbered-mechanism](../section/numbered-mechanism.md).
- The register is elevated.

## Conflicts with
- [numbered-mechanism](../section/numbered-mechanism.md) for the same content.

## Tags
`rhythm: paratactic`, `register: plain`

## Safe or adds
Safe. Adding items not in the source is an add.

## The move
Lay the items out in a run, separated by commas or semicolons, concrete nouns first, no explanation of each. Let the length of the list carry the weight. Close with one sentence that says what they have in common.

## Before
> run their own server, which places the site, the subscriber list, the archive, and the revenue in the hands of whoever holds the credentials.

## After
> Whoever holds the credentials holds the site, the subscriber list, the archive, the revenue, the name readers type, and the links that point at it. The writers hold their drafts.

*("the name readers type, and the links" adds items; drop them unless the author approves.)*

## Pitfalls
- Abstract items ("integrity, trust, sovereignty").
- Lists so long the reader stops reading.

## Combines with
[cumulative-sentence](../sentence/cumulative-sentence.md), [concrete-figures](../extensions/concrete-figures.md)
