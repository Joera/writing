# One-line paragraph

## Use when
- One sentence is the turn of the whole section and must not be read as part of the paragraph around it.
- Once or twice in a document, at most.

## Don't use when
- The register is elevated.
- The sentence is an epigram the reader must decode.

## Conflicts with
- `register: elevated`; [cadence](../sentence/cadence.md) on the same sentence (one or the other does the emphasis).

## Tags
`rhythm: paratactic, short`

## Safe or adds
Safe.

## The move
Set the sentence on its own as a paragraph. Make it short and literal. The white space is the emphasis; the sentence needs none of its own.

## Before
> [...] it follows from the price of leaving. The protocol's move is to separate editorial judgment from the position it currently rides on.

## After
> [...] It follows from the price of leaving.
>
> The protocol's move is to separate editorial judgment from the position it rides on.
>
> An author keeps ownership of the work and can revoke it at any time. [...]

## Pitfalls
- Overuse, which turns the text into a slide deck.
- Using it for a sentence that does not merit it.
