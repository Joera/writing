# Direct address

## Use when
- The document's address is "you" and a paragraph needs the reader to imagine themselves in a position (the writer who wants to leave).
- Essays, posts, talks.

## Don't use when
- The document is impersonal. This move is then out entirely, and stray "you"s are errors to fix (the Polyphony draft has two: "rent space" in section 3, "you cannot battle-test" in section 6).

## Conflicts with
- `address: impersonal`; [outsiders-view](../section/outsiders-view.md) in the same passage.

## Tags
`address: "you"`, `presence: visible`

## Safe or adds
Safe.

## The move
Speak to the reader as "you" and put them in the situation: "You have written for a site for six years. You want to leave." Keep "you" consistent: one reader, not a generic "one".

## Before
> A falling-out costs the writer their income, their record, and the home of their byline.

## After (in a "you" document)
> If you fall out with the site, you lose your income, your record, and the home of your byline. The site loses one contributor.

## Pitfalls
- Generic "you" (meaning "anyone") mixed with direct "you".
- Assuming the reader's position ("you, as a writer…") when many readers are not writers.

## Combines with
[staged-objection](../section/staged-objection.md), [aside](../extensions/aside.md)
