# Build, then land

## Use when
- A paragraph accumulates details toward one point, and the point is currently buried in the middle.
- The paragraph closes a case, a diagnosis or a section.

## Don't use when
- The paragraph's point must come first for skimmers (lead with it; see [one-before-many](one-before-many.md)).
- The paragraph is periodic in design: then the landing is inside the last sentence, not after it.

## Conflicts with
- [periodic-sentence](../sentence/periodic-sentence.md) as the paragraph's dominant rhythm.

## Tags
`rhythm: paratactic`

## Safe or adds
Safe.

## The move
Build with several medium or long sentences that add detail side by side. Then land with one short declarative sentence that states the point. The contrast in length does the work; no "therefore" needed.

## Before
> Whichever version is true, the outcome is the same: years of his work still stand on the site, under the masthead of the owner he is accusing — and whether they stay there, come down, or quietly change bylines rests on the owner's goodwill, not on any right of his own.

## After
> When it finally breaks open, it breaks open on social media, and the two accounts don't meet. He says he was fired for dissent, and that his work was kept and his name managed without him. The owner says he was let go for cause. Whichever account is true, the outcome is the same. Years of his work still stand on the site, under the masthead of the owner he is accusing, and whether they stay, come down, or quietly change bylines depends on the owner's goodwill, not on anything he controls.

## Pitfalls
- A landing sentence that is an epigram rather than the point.
- Landing every paragraph the same way, so it becomes a tic.
