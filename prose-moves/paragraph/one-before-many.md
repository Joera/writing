# One before many

## Use when
- A paragraph opens with a general rule and only then gives instances, and readers lose the rule before they see what it means.
- One concrete instance can stand for the class.

## Don't use when
- The paragraph is a definition, where the general statement must come first.

## Conflicts with
None.

## Tags
None.

## Safe or adds
Safe if the instance is in the source.

## The move
Open on one specific instance. Then generalise: "The same is true of…", "Every decision works this way." The reader learns the rule from the example.

## Before
> Its decisions are acts rather than settings, so admitting a piece after review, whitelisting an author for direct publication, and changing the site's design all go through the same procedure.

## After
> Admitting a piece after review is an act, recorded when it happens. So is whitelisting an author for direct publication, and so is changing the site's design. Every decision a publication makes goes through the same procedure.

## Pitfalls
- An instance so vivid it reads as the whole class.
- An instance that is not typical.
