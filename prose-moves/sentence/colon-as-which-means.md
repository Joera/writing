# Colon as "which means"

## Use when
- The second half of a sentence explains, specifies or follows from the first.
- A long sentence currently joins them with "and", "which", or a dash.

## Don't use when
- The two halves are merely in sequence (use a full stop) or in contrast (use "but").
- The sentence already has a colon.

## Conflicts with
None.

## Tags
None.

## Safe or adds
Safe.

## The move
Join the claim and its explanation with a colon. Read the colon as "which means" or "namely". Semicolons do the same job for two equal claims side by side.

## Before
> Agreements are recorded on a public ledger, and neither party operates the record, neither can rewrite it, and there is no host to pull.

## After
> Agreements are recorded on a public ledger: neither party operates the record, neither can rewrite it, and there is no host to pull.

## Pitfalls
- Colons everywhere, so they stop signalling.
- A colon followed by something that does not explain what came before.
