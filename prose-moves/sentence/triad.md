# Triad

## Use when
- There are genuinely three items, and listing them in rising order of weight helps the last land.

## Don't use when
- There are two or four items (don't pad or cut to three).
- Triads appear in the surrounding paragraphs already.

## Conflicts with
- [balanced-pair](balanced-pair.md) in the same sentence.

## Tags
None.

## Safe or adds
Safe if the three items are in the source.

## The move
List three items, parallel in form, the shortest or least weighty first and the heaviest last. No "and" before the first two; one before the last.

## Before
> A falling-out costs the writer their income, their record, and the home of their byline

## After
> A falling-out costs the writer their income, their record and the home of their byline.

*(Already a triad; the move confirms order: the byline's home is heaviest and is last.)*

## Pitfalls
- Reflexive triads; a reader soon hears the rhythm and stops believing it.
- A third item added to complete the pattern.

## Combines with
[cadence](cadence.md), [catalogue-paragraph](../paragraph/catalogue-paragraph.md)
