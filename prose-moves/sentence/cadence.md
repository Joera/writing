# Cadence

## Use when
- The last sentence of a section or of the document should land when read aloud.
- The document's rhythm is "cadenced at section closes" or fully cadenced.

## Don't use when
- Mid-paragraph in a paratactic document.
- The sentence is technical and its order is fixed by meaning.

## Conflicts with
- [one-line-paragraph](../paragraph/one-line-paragraph.md) on the same sentence.

## Tags
`rhythm: cadenced` (or cadenced at section closes).

## Safe or adds
Safe: only order and sound change, never meaning.

## The move
Read the sentence aloud. Put the stressed, weighty word last. Prefer a falling end (stressed syllable followed by one or two unstressed ones, or a strong monosyllable after a run of lighter words). Cut syllables that trip. Vary the length of the sentence before it so this one stands out.

## Before
> An editorial line held by people free to leave means something a captive one cannot.

## After
> An editorial line held by people free to leave means something a captive one cannot.

*(Unchanged: it already ends on the stressed word. This is the Polyphony paper's one deliberate repetition, and the test is that it survives reading aloud.)*

## Pitfalls
- Cadence that makes a sentence sound true rather than be true.
- Cadence everywhere; it then reads as incantation.
