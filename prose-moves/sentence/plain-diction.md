# Plain diction

## Use when
- A sentence uses abstract, Latinate or jargon words where plain ones exist.
- The register is plain.

## Don't use when
- The technical term is the precise one and has been defined (keep "content-addressed", "threshold").
- The register is elevated by choice.

## Conflicts with
- `register: elevated`.

## Tags
`register: plain`

## Safe or adds
Safe, if the plain word means the same thing. If it doesn't, keep the original.

## The move
Swap abstractions for concrete words, nominalisations for verbs, and long words for short ones where the meaning holds. "Accumulated, already-published work" → "published work". "Is prone to the risks of" → "can be".

## Before
> An author who leaves takes not their future contributions but their accumulated, already-published work off the publication's live vouched record

## After
> An author who leaves takes their published work off the publication's record

*(Check that "live vouched" is defined elsewhere before cutting; if it is a term, keep it.)*

## Pitfalls
- Replacing a defined term with a looser everyday word (breaks [term-discipline](term-discipline.md)).
- Plainness that becomes vague.
